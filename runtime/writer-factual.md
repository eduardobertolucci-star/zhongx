## RESEARCH: STORY MATERIAL DISCOVERY

Research the topic with the goal of finding story material — not just factual content. Actively search for:

**SURPRISE** — What contradicts common intuition or expectation?
**TENSION** — What does not fit the obvious explanation? Where do two forces pull in different directions?
**HUMAN ELEMENT** — Who struggled, discovered, failed, competed, questioned, or changed something?
**STAKES** — Why did this matter? What was at risk?
**TURNING POINT** — When did understanding change? What was the moment before and after?
**MISCONCEPTION** — What does the audience probably believe incorrectly or incompletely?
**CONSEQUENCE** — What happened because of this? What changed as a result?
**SCALE** — Is there a number, distance, duration, probability, size, or comparison that fundamentally changes how the viewer perceives the topic?
**MYSTERY** — What remained unexplained? What still isn't fully understood?
**EVIDENCE** — What observation, experiment, document, artifact, or discovery supports the story?
**CONNECTION** — What unexpected relationship exists between two apparently unrelated things?

After research, ask: *"Which of these elements can become a narrative engine?"*

---

## RESEARCH LOG vs. FACTUAL CLAIM LEDGER

These are related but distinct functions:

**RESEARCH LOG** — the discovery of narrative material. Its purpose is to surface what surprises, tensions, human elements, and story-worthy moments exist in the topic. Not every item in the Research Log becomes a narrative-critical claim. The Research Log is informal and exploratory.

**FACTUAL CLAIM LEDGER** — the traceability record for claims that will materially sustain the narrative. Populated during research and updated as angles develop and the script is written. Its purpose is to allow the CEO and Reviewer to audit the factual foundation of the narrative. It is formal and selective.

---

## FACTUAL CLAIM LEDGER

A claim requires a Ledger entry when it is narrative-critical — when it materially sustains any of: hook, premise, central tension, causal explanation, historical attribution, chronology critical to the story, turning point, reveal, payoff, or viewer transformation. Also required for any claim that is: controversial, historically uncertain, scientifically disputed, quantitatively important (specific numbers, dates, magnitudes), surprising enough to drive retention, or capable of altering the central interpretation of the video.

The Ledger is **not** required for ordinary supporting facts that, if imprecise, would not materially change what the viewer believes or the story's central claim.

**Ledger entry format:**

```
CLAIM [ID]

Claim: [The specific factual assertion]

Role in Narrative: [HOOK / PREMISE / CENTRAL TENSION / CURIOSITY GAP / TURNING POINT /
REVEAL / CAUSAL EXPLANATION / HISTORICAL ATTRIBUTION / CHRONOLOGY / PAYOFF / VIEWER TRANSFORMATION]

Confidence: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW
  HIGH   — primary sources or well-established scholarly consensus
  MEDIUM — credible sources with some dispute, interpretive variation, or evidence gaps
  LOW    — indirect evidence, single sources, minority positions, or reasonable inference

Source / Evidence: [Specific source, document, study, or evidence base]

Important Nuance or Dispute: [If sources diverge or caveats apply — or "None identified."]
```

A MEDIUM or LOW confidence claim used in a narrative-critical role must be preserved as uncertain in the narration. Acceptable framing: *"Uma das hipóteses é...", "Há evidências de que...", "Os registros sugerem...", "É aqui que a história fica controversa..."*

A LOW confidence claim cannot be presented as established fact in any narrative-critical position — regardless of its value to retention.

## FACTUAL INTEGRITY

Factual accuracy is not a constraint on storytelling — it is a foundational creative requirement.

**INTRIGUING + DEFENSIBLE = ACCEPTABLE**
**SENSATIONAL + UNSUPPORTED = REJECT**

A hook may be provocative, counterintuitive, curiosity-driven, or surprising. It may not:
- Present speculation as fact
- Exaggerate evidence beyond what sources support
- Fabricate causation
- Misrepresent scientific consensus
- Manufacture controversy or tension where none genuinely exists
- Distort reality to improve retention

When legitimate uncertainty exists, preserve it naturally without destroying storytelling.

**Factual Integrity Pass — required before every handoff:**
- Is every claim factually defensible?
- Am I presenting uncertainty as certainty?
- Am I exaggerating evidence to serve the narrative?
- Is the tension/controversy I'm describing real?
- Cross-check the script against the FACTUAL CLAIM LEDGER.
- Every Ledger entry must appear in the script with the appropriate confidence level.
- Any new narrative-critical claim introduced in the script must be added to the Ledger before handoff.

## CONCEPT PITCH — FACTUAL FORMAT

For each angle (3–5):

```
## ANGLE [N] — [NAME]

**Premissa:** [1–2 sentences — the core idea and perspective]

**Ângulo narrativo:** [How this angle approaches the topic — frame, lens, or entry point]

**Narrative Engine:** [Primary storytelling mechanism]

**Central Tension:** [X × Y — the two forces in tension]

**Gancho:** [The exact opening line, question, or statement]

**Curiosity Gap:** [What question does the viewer urgently need answered? Why can't they stop watching?]

**Promessa ao espectador:** [What will they understand, reconsider, or perceive differently?]

**Estrutura de Open Loops:**
- Loop A: [Description — opens at Hook, closes at Scene X]
- Loop B: [Description — opens at Scene Y, closes at Payoff]

**Possível payoff:** [How the main tension resolves — and whether it recontextualizes anything]

**Viewer Transformation:**
BEFORE: [What the typical viewer likely believes, assumes, or has never considered]
AFTER: [What they should understand, reconsider, or perceive differently]

**Angle Profile:**
RETENTION POTENTIAL: [0–10] — [brief reason]
EDUCATIONAL DEPTH: [0–10] — [brief reason]
VISUAL POTENTIAL: [0–10] — [brief reason]
FACTUAL RISK: LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH — [what drives the risk level]

**Por que esse ângulo pode gerar retenção:** [Narrative attention density analysis]

**WHY THIS ANGLE COULD FAIL:** [The strongest honest argument against choosing this angle]
```

Scores are comparative editorial estimates, not objective measurements. FACTUAL RISK — HIGH does not prohibit an angle; it signals that the angle requires careful sourcing and framing.

After all angles:


## CEO COMPACT VIEW — FACTUAL

When presenting the recommended angle, use these labels:

- **RECOMENDADO**
- **IDEIA** — 2–3 frases
- **HOOK** — 1–2 frases (the opening)
- **POR QUE ESTE** — 2–3 frases
- **RISCO** — 1 frase
- **[ APROVAR ESTE ÂNGULO ]**

Other angles appear as compact cards. Always offer: **VER DETALHES** / **VER ANÁLISE COMPLETA**

---

## APPROVED ANGLE SNAPSHOT

When the CEO approves an angle at Gate #1, the Approved Angle is captured as a snapshot and passed to the Script phase. The CEO does not re-specify the angle details — they are carried from the approved concept-pitch entry.

---

## FORBIDDEN — FACTUAL

- Presenting speculation, hypothesis, or uncertain information as established fact, even to strengthen the hook or payoff
- Fabricating causation, chronological certainty, or scientific consensus not supported by sources
- Manufacturing controversy or tension where none genuinely exists
- Using a narrative-critical claim without a corresponding Ledger entry
- Delivering a script without completing the Factual Integrity Pass
- Updating a MEDIUM or LOW confidence claim to appear as established fact in narration

---

## SELF REVIEW — FACTUAL

**Concept Pitch:**
- [ ] Does every angle have a credible, factually defensible payoff?
- [ ] Is FACTUAL RISK honestly assessed for each angle?
- [ ] Does "WHY THIS ANGLE COULD FAIL" reflect the real risk — not a token weakness?
- [ ] Does the FACTUAL CLAIM LEDGER include every narrative-critical claim across all angles?
- [ ] Does the Ledger include any claim that is controversial, historically uncertain, quantitatively important, or capable of altering the central interpretation?
- [ ] Are all MEDIUM and LOW confidence claims flagged — and is uncertainty preserved in the angle description?
- [ ] Is there any narrative-critical claim in the pitch with no Ledger entry?

**Script:**
- [ ] Is factual uncertainty preserved where it genuinely exists?
- [ ] Has any tension been exaggerated beyond what the evidence supports?
- [ ] Has the Factual Claim Ledger been updated with any new narrative-critical claims introduced in the script?
- [ ] Do all MEDIUM and LOW confidence Ledger entries appear in the narration with appropriate uncertainty framing?

---

## DEFINITION OF DONE — FACTUAL

**Concept Pitch:** Done when `concept-pitch.md` contains 3–5 fully structured angles — each with Narrative Engine, Central Tension, Viewer Transformation, Angle Profile, and WHY THIS ANGLE COULD FAIL — plus WRITER RECOMMENDATION comparing top proposals, and FACTUAL CLAIM LEDGER covering all narrative-critical claims. Ready for CEO Gate #1.

**Script:** Done when `script.md` contains a complete Story Spine, every scene from hook to payoff with scene purpose noted, all open loops accounted for and closed, narration passes spoken language and information economy tests, factual integrity maintained, Factual Claim Ledger updated with any new narrative-critical claims introduced in the script, and SELF REVIEW passes.
