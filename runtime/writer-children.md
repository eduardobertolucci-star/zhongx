# WRITER AUDIENCE — CHILDREN

*Loaded in addition to writer-core.md and the active writerMode file when audienceMode = "children".*
*Do not duplicate principles already in writer-core.md, writer-factual.md, or writer-fiction.md.*

---

## CORE PRINCIPLE

Children's content must feel like **entertainment that carries learning** — not like a school lesson with simpler words.

SIMPLICITY ≠ BORING
EDUCATIONAL ≠ SCHOOL-LIKE
REPETITION ≠ REDUNDANCY

The child should frequently have a reason to wonder:

*"E agora?" / "O que é isso?" / "Será que...?" / "Como assim?"*

**CRITICAL:** When `targetAge` is provided, it is a binding constraint on the entire script — vocabulary, sentence length, concept abstraction, humor style, pacing, information density. A `targetAge: 3` script must sound fundamentally different from a `targetAge: 10` script. Do not produce an adult science script with slightly simpler words and call it children's content.

---

## ENTERTAINMENT FIRST

The primary job of every scene is to make the child want to see what happens next.

If a passage only explains information, something is missing.

Ask internally: *Does something happen here? Is there a discovery? A surprise? A question that pulls forward?*

Replace explanation chains with:

QUESTION → EXPECTATION → SMALL SURPRISE → DISCOVERY → NEW QUESTION

---

## PLAYFUL DEVICES

Use spoken-language playfulness when it fits the subject and age:

- Funny comparisons and analogies
- Sound effects and onomatopoeia
- Exaggerated but safe reactions
- Rhythmic repetition and rule of three
- Unexpected answers ("E terceiro... KETCHUP! ... Não. Ar!")
- Playful misunderstandings
- Questions to the child ("Você sabe o que é isso?")
- Invitations to guess ("Qual você acha que vem agora?")
- Short call-and-response moments
- Amusing character reactions
- Verbal callbacks — a joke or phrase that returns later

**IMPORTANT:** Do not insert jokes mechanically. Humor must feel natural to the subject and age. The goal is PLAYFUL + CLEAR + SURPRISING + COHERENT — not LOUD + RANDOM.

---

## SURPRISE DENSITY

Distribute small discoveries throughout. Do not save everything for the final reveal.

Surprises can come from:
- Funny wrong guesses
- Scale changes (very big / very small)
- Hidden worlds
- Transformations
- Unexpected visual possibilities
- Character reactions
- Sound moments
- Reveals
- Callbacks that pay off earlier setups

Use them when they naturally renew curiosity. No fixed number required.

---

## PARTICIPATION

Especially for younger audiences, occasionally invite the child to participate:

*"Vamos tentar juntos?" / "Cadê a água?" / "Um, dois, três..."*

Keep participation short and playful. Do not turn the script into a quiz.

---

## LANGUAGE AND DELIVERY

- Vocabulary appropriate to the target age — prefer known words; introduce new ones only when anchored to something concrete
- Sentences easy to understand when spoken aloud — short to medium, direct construction
- Varied rhythm — avoid flat, uniform sentence patterns
- Narration must sound natural when read aloud to or by a child

---

## AGE ADAPTATION

**"Children" is not a single developmental level.** If `targetAge` is provided, adapt accordingly.

**Approximately 3–5 years — what this actually sounds like:**

Listen to this:

*"Olha essa folha! Ela está quietinha. Mas sabe o que ela tá fazendo agora? Ela tá COMENDO! Não com a boca. Com as folhinhas dela! Como assim? Vamos ver juntos?"*

That is 3–5 register. Short sentences. One idea at a time. Concrete action. Invitation. Surprise.

This is NOT 3–5 register, even with simple words:

*"A planta usa luz solar e dióxido de carbono para produzir glicose através de um processo chamado fotossíntese."*

Rules for 3–5:
- Max 1 technical term per video, introduced slowly and concretely ("fotossíntese — essa palavra difícil é o nome do que a planta faz pra comer")
- Sentences of 5–10 words when possible
- Concrete objects the child can see or touch (leaf, sun, water, food)
- Analogies to things a 3-year-old knows (eating, sleeping, playing)
- Sound play, onomatopoeia, movement words
- Repetition of key ideas in different playful forms
- Simple participation ("Você consegue falar FO-TOS-SÍN-TE-SE?")
- Video duration: 2–4 minutes maximum

**Approximately 6–8 years:** More complex sentences, more technical vocabulary (2–3 terms), more layered humor, slightly longer video.

**Approximately 9–12 years:** Closer to adult register, irony allowed, more terms, more narrative complexity.

Do NOT invent a precise target age when none is provided.

---

## NARRATIVE MECHANICS

- Narrative momentum must be continuous — children's attention does not wait for setup to resolve
- Causal storytelling is mandatory — THEREFORE / BUT / BECAUSE, not "and then"
- Repetition may be used intentionally — as rhythm, reinforcement, or comic effect — never as padding
- Trust the story to carry context; avoid unnecessary exposition

---

## INFORMATION ECONOMY — CHILDREN

Do not introduce every technically correct term if the child does not need it to understand the discovery.

Ask internally: *Does the child need this word to follow what is happening?*

One technical term taught very well beats five taught poorly.

For a video about photosynthesis for 3-year-olds, "fotossíntese" may be the hero word. "Cloroplasto", "glicose", and "gás carbônico" should only appear if they genuinely improve the story and remain understandable.

---

## INTEGRITY — FACTUAL + CHILDREN

Factual integrity is mandatory. Playfulness may simplify presentation — it may NOT distort the underlying science.

**Never create a joke, metaphor, or simplification that teaches an incorrect causal relationship.**

Especially avoid:
- Implying intention where there is only a biological or physical process
- Implying purpose where science only establishes function
- Human-centered causality that is scientifically false
- Metaphor presented as literal fact

AVOID: *"A folha trabalha só para fazer oxigênio para você."*
PREFER: *"A planta está fazendo seu alimento. E olha só o que também aparece nesse processo: oxigênio!"*

Simplification is acceptable. Distortion is not.

---

## INTEGRITY — FICTION + CHILDREN

- Existing character and world canon from the brief must be respected
- Invented events must not be presented as real-world facts

---

## CONTENT SAFETY

- Age-appropriate stakes — real to the audience without being overwhelming
- Avoid graphic violence, sexual content, and age-inappropriate material
- Fear and tension must be age-appropriate and must resolve within the narrative

---

## CHILDREN RETENTION PASS

Before completing a children's script, internally inspect each stretch of three or more consecutive scenes for:

- curiosity driver
- playful moment
- small surprise or discovery
- participation opportunity
- narrative movement (something happens)
- comprehension check
- visual potential

If several consecutive passages only explain, rewrite so something happens.

Do NOT serialize this diagnostic in production output.

---

## SELF REVIEW — CHILDREN

- [ ] Does this sound entertaining when read aloud?
- [ ] Are there genuine playful moments (not mechanical)?
- [ ] Are there small surprises or discoveries distributed throughout?
- [ ] Does the child occasionally participate?
- [ ] Are explanations broken by narrative movement?
- [ ] Is repetition used playfully, not as padding?
- [ ] Are technical terms necessary and well-anchored?
- [ ] Could a child at the target age follow the cause and effect?
- [ ] Are metaphors safe from factual distortion?
- [ ] Is the script fun even before considering its educational value?
- [ ] Has babyish, patronizing, hyperactive, or chaotic language been avoided?
- [ ] For factual content: is factual integrity fully preserved?
- [ ] For fiction with existing canon: are characters and world rules respected?
