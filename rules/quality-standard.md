# Quality Standard — Scoring System

**ZhongX Studio — Review & Evaluation Reference**

---

## THE STUDIO SCORE

The Studio Score is the definitive measure of production readiness before CEO Final Gate authorization.

**Minimum Studio Score for CEO Final Gate: 8.5 / 10**

Studio Score = arithmetic mean of all 8 dimension scores.

No dimension override is permitted. A video cannot compensate for a weak dimension with excellence in another. Every dimension must be addressed.

---

## 8 EVALUATION DIMENSIONS

### 1. HOOK (0–10)

**What is evaluated:**
- Does the opening create an immediate, genuine curiosity gap?
- Is the hook honest — does it represent content that actually exists in the video?
- Does it make a clear, credible promise proportional to the topic?
- Does it feel personally relevant to the specific target audience?
- Does maximum tension arrive within 30 seconds?

**Scoring guide:**

| Score | What it means |
|-------|--------------|
| 9–10 | Irresistible. Creates urgent need to know the answer. Specific, honest, platform-appropriate. A viewer would stop scrolling. |
| 7–8 | Effective. Creates genuine interest but could be sharper, faster, or more specific. |
| 5–6 | Functional but generic. Creates mild interest without urgency. High early drop-off risk. |
| < 5 | Fails to create curiosity, overpromises relative to content, or misrepresents the video. |

---

### 2. RETENÇÃO (0–10)

**What is evaluated:**
- Are open loops used effectively and resolved appropriately?
- Are re-hooks present at approximately 60–90 second intervals?
- Does every scene give the viewer a meaningful reason to continue?
- Is there a progressive sense of escalating insight or revelation?
- Does the video avoid dead zones (>90 seconds without retention event)?

**Scoring guide:**

| Score | What it means |
|-------|--------------|
| 9–10 | Strong, deliberate open loop architecture. Regular re-hooks. No dead zones. Viewer always has a forward-pulling question. |
| 7–8 | Effective retention design with 1–2 gaps or weak re-hooks. |
| 5–6 | Some open loops but insufficient re-hooks. Significant mid-video drop-off risk. |
| < 5 | No deliberate retention architecture. Feels like a sequence of facts without tension. |

---

### 3. STORYTELLING (0–10)

**What is evaluated:**
- Is the narrative arc clear and emotionally satisfying?
- Does the progression feel logical — and in hindsight, inevitable?
- Are scene transitions narratively motivated, not arbitrary?
- Does the payoff directly and fully deliver on the narrative promise?
- Is the emotional journey (curiosity → engagement → surprise → satisfaction) deliberately designed?

**Scoring guide:**

| Score | What it means |
|-------|--------------|
| 9–10 | Compelling narrative creating genuine emotional investment. Every scene serves the arc. The payoff is earned. |
| 7–8 | Clear structure with strong moments. Some scenes feel loosely connected. |
| 5–6 | Structure is present but mechanical. Limited emotional engagement. |
| < 5 | No clear narrative arc. Feels like a list of facts, not a story. |

---

### 4. CLAREZA (0–10)

**What is evaluated:**
- Is every concept explained in a way the target audience can understand?
- Are analogies effective and — critically — accurate?
- Is terminology appropriate for the audience without being condescending?
- Does the video avoid false simplifications that introduce incorrect beliefs?
- Would a viewer with no prior knowledge of the topic understand the key ideas?

**Scoring guide:**

| Score | What it means |
|-------|--------------|
| 9–10 | Maximum clarity without sacrificing accuracy. Complex ideas feel genuinely accessible. Analogies illuminate without misleading. |
| 7–8 | Clear overall with minor moments of unnecessary complexity or mild oversimplification. |
| 5–6 | Some concepts are unclear or poorly explained. Viewers may leave confused about key ideas. |
| < 5 | Significant clarity failures. Target audience would not understand key content, or content is simplified to the point of distortion. |

---

### 5. VISUAL (0–10)

**What is evaluated:**
- Does the storyboard effectively translate the narrative into visual language?
- Is the visual style consistent and appropriate for the content and tone?
- Do visuals add meaning, or merely decorate?
- Is on-screen text used purposefully and legibly?
- Are asset prompts specific and production-ready?
- Does the edit-guide execute the storyboard's visual intent?
- **Is visual attention adequately renewed?** Evaluate contextually — not by fixed interval. A beat may hold legitimately when internal motion, progressive annotations, zoom/pan, or narrative stability sustains attention. A beat fails when viewer attention would decay without a visual reason to continue.
- Does the beat breakdown (sub-scenes) reflect genuine rhythm and retention decisions, informed by narration timing and content density?

**Scoring guide:**

| Score | What it means |
|-------|--------------|
| 9–10 | Visual language amplifies the narrative. Consistent. Purposeful. Production-ready. Visual attention density is well-calibrated to narration rhythm and content. |
| 7–8 | Effective with minor inconsistencies or 1–2 beats where attention renewal is weak. |
| 5–6 | Visuals present but not well-integrated with narration rhythm. Multiple beats where attention renewal is absent or unjustified. |
| < 5 | Visual language contradicts or ignores narrative. Major style inconsistencies. Sustained visual monotony without justification. |

---

### 6. RITMO (0–10)

**What is evaluated:**
- Does the production achieve the right pace at every moment?
- Are energy peaks (hook, re-hooks, revelation, payoff) properly amplified by pacing and sound design?
- Are there deliberate breathing points that allow complex ideas to land before moving on?
- Is the audio-visual synchronization precise and intentional?
- Does the pacing feel human and natural, or mechanical and arbitrary?

**Scoring guide:**

| Score | What it means |
|-------|--------------|
| 9–10 | Masterful pacing. The video breathes. Energy is controlled precisely. Synchronization is seamless. |
| 7–8 | Good overall pacing with 1–2 flat zones or rushed moments. |
| 5–6 | Inconsistent pacing. Some scenes are too dense; others too slow. Sync issues in notable places. |
| < 5 | Pacing failures that significantly undermine the viewing experience. Major sync issues. |

---

### 7. PAYOFF (0–10)

**What is evaluated:**
- Does the conclusion directly and satisfyingly answer the hook question?
- Is the payoff proportional to the setup? (Big hook = payoff must be bigger)
- Does the viewer leave with a clear, memorable, distinct takeaway?
- Is the CTA (if present) placed correctly — after the payoff has fully landed?
- Does the payoff create an emotional response (satisfaction, surprise, inspiration, changed perspective)?

**Scoring guide:**

| Score | What it means |
|-------|--------------|
| 9–10 | The payoff is worth the full runtime. Leaves viewers satisfied, changed, and wanting to share. |
| 7–8 | Solid payoff that delivers on the promise. Minor room for more emotional impact. |
| 5–6 | Payoff exists but underdelivers. The promise created more tension than the answer resolves. |
| < 5 | Payoff is missing, weak, or arrives before the viewer is emotionally ready. Main loop not explicitly closed. |

---

### 8. PRECISÃO (0–10)

**What is evaluated:**

Distinguish between two categories:

**A) Ordinary factual detail** — supporting information that, if imprecise, would not materially change what the viewer believes or the story's central claim.

**B) Narrative-critical factual claim** — any fact that supports the hook, establishes the central thesis, creates controversy, establishes causation or chronology essential to the story, creates the payoff, or materially determines what the viewer is meant to believe.

- Are all claims (A and B) accurate and verifiable?
- Are numbers, dates, names, and attributions correct?
- Are necessary nuances and caveats included where the topic demands them?
- Does the video avoid misrepresenting scientific consensus or historical record?
- Are analogies factually accurate — do they illuminate without introducing incorrect beliefs?
- Are all narrative-critical claims (B) fully defensible? Speculation or contested information framed as established fact is not acceptable even if it improves the hook or payoff.

**Scoring guide:**

| Score | What it means |
|-------|--------------|
| 9–10 | Fully accurate, ordinary and narrative-critical. Nuanced where required. Every claim is defensible. |
| 7–8 | Accurate overall with minor imprecisions in ordinary detail that do not distort the core message. |
| 5–6 | Some factual imprecisions — including in narrative-supporting claims — that could mislead the audience. |
| < 5 | Significant factual errors or unsupported narrative-critical claims that damage credibility or introduce false beliefs. |

**Special rule:** An unsupported narrative-critical factual claim (Category B) triggers a GRAVE issue regardless of the overall Studio Score. A strong hook does not compensate for a factual foundation that is not defensible.

---

## STUDIO SCORE CALCULATION

```
Studio Score = (HOOK + RETENÇÃO + STORYTELLING + CLAREZA + VISUAL + RITMO + PAYOFF + PRECISÃO) ÷ 8
```

**Thresholds:**

| Studio Score | Status |
|-------------|--------|
| 9.0 – 10.0 | EXCEPTIONAL — CEO Final Gate: recommended with no conditions |
| 8.5 – 8.9 | APPROVED — CEO Final Gate: recommended |
| 7.0 – 8.4 | CORRECTIONS REQUIRED — targeted corrections, mandatory re-review |
| < 7.0 | GRAVE — CEO Extraordinary Gate required before any correction proceeds |

---

## SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION

### LEVE
- **Score impact:** Less than 0.5 points per dimension
- **Scope:** Localized — does not affect narrative, strategy, or factual accuracy
- **Resolution:** Direct routing to responsible agent
- **CEO notification:** No
- **Examples:** Missing pronunciation annotation for one term; one SFX cue undefined; small typographic inconsistency

---

### MODERADO
- **Score impact:** 0.5–1.5 points per dimension
- **Scope:** Affects quality of one element meaningfully but does not require structural rework
- **Resolution:** Direct routing to responsible agent
- **CEO notification:** In final `review.md` only
- **Examples:** Hook lacks sufficient urgency; one scene's storyboard is visually unclear; music arc has a dead zone; tone inconsistent across two scenes

---

### GRAVE — CEO EXTRAORDINARY GATE REQUIRED
- **Production status:** HALTED
- **Resolution:** CEO decision required before any correction proceeds
- **CEO notification:** Immediate — ESCALATION REPORT delivered to CEO

**A problem is classified as GRAVE when any of the following is true:**
- Any single dimension score is below 7
- Studio Score is below 7
- Factual error that is significant and verifiable
- Unsupported narrative-critical factual claim (Category B in PRECISÃO) — regardless of Studio Score
- Problem undermines or directly contradicts the central premise of the video
- Proposed correction would require altering a decision previously approved by the CEO
- Structural problem requiring significant reconstruction of the video
- Reputational risk — legal exposure, ethical violation, or material factual distortion
- Severe inconsistency between script, narration, and visual that makes the video incoherent

---

## RE-REVIEW PROTOCOL

When corrections are made and a new version of any document is delivered:

1. Reviewer performs a full re-review — not a partial check of only the corrected sections
2. Previous `review.md` is archived to `/history/`
3. A new `review.md` is produced with fresh scores and fresh Problem Reports
4. The Studio Score is recalculated from scratch
5. The same thresholds apply — a re-review does not receive lower standards

A production may go through multiple review cycles. Each cycle is a full review.
